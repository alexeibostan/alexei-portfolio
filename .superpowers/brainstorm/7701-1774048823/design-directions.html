Scroll

A — Cinematic Scroll

Dark, atmospheric, scroll-driven storytelling. One continuous flow. Sections reveal with parallax and fade-in. Feels like a documentary — each scroll reveals the next chapter. Moody palette with warm gold accents. Serif typography for editorial weight.

Strengths

  • Maximally immersive and memorable
  • Scroll animations showcase frontend craft
  • Strong emotional impact

Trade-offs

  • Heavier to build (scroll orchestration)
  • Recruiters who want to skim may need a "jump to" option

B — Architectural Precision

Light, grid-driven, Swiss-inspired with revealing transitions. Hybrid structure: a scrollable homepage with detail pages. Sections slide and assemble like architectural blueprints. Numbered navigation (01, 02, 03). Restraint is the statement — every element earns its place.

Strengths

  • Extremely clean and scannable for recruiters
  • Grid animations still showcase craft
  • Timeless — won't feel dated

Trade-offs

  • Less emotionally dramatic than dark themes
  • Restraint requires more design discipline

C — Developer Narrative

Dark dev-aesthetic with rich interactivity. GitHub-inspired palette meets portfolio storytelling. Terminal hints without being gimmicky. Interactive project cards with live-feeling hover states and smooth page transitions. Monospace accents signal engineering identity. The site feels like exploring a well-crafted codebase.

Strengths

  • Immediately signals "engineer" to technical leads
  • Dark theme naturally feels modern
  • Familiar to developer audience

Trade-offs

  • Risk of feeling too "developer portfolio" if not elevated
  • May not stand out to non-technical recruiters

My recommendation: Direction A (Cinematic Scroll) — it's the most distinctive and best demonstrates frontend craft through the experience itself. But B and C are strong choices too. You can also combine elements (e.g., A's scroll storytelling with C's dark developer palette).
